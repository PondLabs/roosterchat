Voice rooms

Drop in. Hang out.

A voice room is a room, not a call. Nobody rings: it's open whenever someone's in it, and you can see who is before you pull up a chair.

  1. Voice rooms that stay open

    Rooms don't ring, they just are. Drop in, drop out, and see who's around before you join. Set each person's volume, use push to talk if you like, and let noise suppression take the dishwasher out of your mic.

  2. Share your screen, with its sound

    Stream a game, or put a movie on for everyone. You pick the bitrate and frame rate, and nobody asks you to pay for 1080p. When several people share, watch them side by side.

  3. A soundboard in every call

    Six classics live under the rooster in every call. Add your house's own from a file or a link, trim them in the editor, and everyone hears them at the same loudness. No subscription to use them anywhere.

  4. Take the decks

    Queue songs from your own files and play them into the room. Everyone hears the same song at the same moment, late joiners included, and each person sets their own volume. Source extensions you install can add more.

  5. Text channels for between rounds

    Replies, reactions, polls, threads, GIFs, custom emoji and stickers, in channels you sort under your own headings. Instagram reels play right in the chat, and X posts open as full cards.

The little things

Small things that make it home.

Noise suppression that stays on your machine

DeepFilterNet3 cleans up your microphone on your own computer, or in your browser, before anything leaves it. Keyboards, fans and the dishwasher stay home.

Your mic
What the room hears

Calls that fix themselves

Every second, Rooster checks that your mic still gets through and that you still hear everyone. If not, it repairs the connection without a rejoin.

Who's around

See who's online, away or in a call, right under your houses, and the quiet rooms you could pull up a chair in.

Controls outside the window

Mute and hang up from the taskbar on Windows, the Dock on macOS, the launcher on Linux, or a small floating panel in the browser.

Your own sounds, trimmed

Add a clip from a file, a link to an audio file, or a page a source extension knows, and trim it to the good part.

Rooster vs Discord

Same hangout. Different landlord.

Rooster has the voice rooms, soundboard, screen sharing and text channels you know from Discord. What's different is who owns the house, and what it costs to make yourself at home.

Questions

Before you move in

Accounts, servers, phones and what it costs.

Do I need to run my own server?

No. Sign in with any Matrix account, from matrix.org or any other homeserver, or make one there. If your crew wants a house of its own, run a homeserver such as Synapse, with LiveKit and its JWT service beside it for the voice rooms (the same setup Element Call uses).

Is it really free?

Yes. There's no paid tier, no ads and no tracking. Rooster is open source under the AGPL-3.0. Things like upload size are set by your server, not by a plan.

Can I use it on my phone?

Not from an app store yet. Open Rooster in your phone's browser and add it to your home screen: it opens like an app, and voice rooms work there too.

Can I talk to friends on Element or another Matrix app?

Yes. Rooms, DMs and spaces are plain Matrix, so they work across apps and servers. Rooster's extras, like the soundboard and the DJ booth, show up for people using Rooster.

What's encrypted?

Messages in encrypted rooms and DMs are end-to-end encrypted, and you can verify each other's devices with emoji. Calls are encrypted on the way to the server. End-to-end encrypted voice rooms are still an experiment you can turn on in Settings → Experiments.

Why does macOS say it can't open Rooster?

The macOS build isn't signed by Apple yet. The first time, right click Rooster and choose Open. After that it opens normally.
